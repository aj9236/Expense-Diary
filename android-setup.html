Set up your Expense Diary

Your records will be kept in your own Google account. Only you can see them. This takes about ten minutes, once. Use Chrome on this phone, signed in with your Google account.

1

Open the service and make your copy

Open in Chrome

If the page looks cramped or asks for a computer, tap Chrome's ⋮ menu and tick Desktop site.

Tap the Make a copy button (two overlapping pages, at the top right). Your own copy opens.

2

Run setup and allow access

In your copy, make sure setup is chosen next to Run, then tap Run.

Tap Review permissions and choose your account. Google warns that the app isn't verified, because it is your own private copy: tap Advanced, then Go to … (unsafe), tick Select all and tap Continue.

Wait until the log shows "Execution completed". A Google Sheet named "Expense Diary" is created in your Drive.

3

Publish it for your phone

Tap Deploy, then New deployment. Tap the gear next to "Select type" and choose Web app.

Set Who has access to Anyone and tap Deploy. If Google asks again, allow access as in step 2.

Copy the Web app URL that appears.

4

Connect the app

Paste the address into Chrome's address bar, add ?connect at the very end, and open it.

Tap Connect the Android app and choose Expense Diary if asked. The app opens, already connected.

5

Allow SMS reading

The app asks to read SMS. Tap Allow. Your bank SMS from the last 30 days then appear on the Confirm page.

If Android says the setting is restricted: open Settings → Apps → Expense Diary, tap the ⋮ menu at the top right, choose Allow restricted settings, then return to the app and tap Allow SMS reading.

Only messages that show an amount in Rs, INR or ₹ are read. Personal messages stay on the phone. Nothing is added to your diary until you tick it and tap Add.
